Model and method

A claim-occurrence classifier on 678,013 French motor policies, wrapped in a dual-explanation engine.

Clean

freMTPL2freq with ClaimNb and IDpol removed. Target is 1 when ClaimNb > 0.

Prepare

Median imputation and scaling for 6 numeric factors; mode imputation and one-hot for 4 categorical factors.

Train

Random Forest, 150 trees, leaf size 5, balanced subsample weights.

Explain

Exact Shapley over 10 factors plus a LIME surrogate. Global SHAP from the training notebook.

Communicate

Top-three adverse factors become a plain-language notice and a compliance report.

Model comparison

Held-out test set

Random Forest was selected for its ROC-AUC and precision. Logistic Regression catches more claims (recall 0.62) but at a precision of only 0.07, so most of its flags are false alarms.

Marginal contribution

What “SHAP” computes

φᵢ = ΣS ⊆ N∖{i} [ |S|! (n−|S|−1)! / n! ] · ( v(S ∪ {i}) − v(S) )

Each factor's contribution is its average marginal effect over every possible coalition of the other factors. With ten factors that is 1,024 coalitions, so this app evaluates the formula exactly against 24 reference policies instead of approximating it. The contributions always sum to the gap between the applicant's score and the baseline.

Global and local, together

Global SHAP summarises the whole portfolio so compliance can test for discriminatory reliance on age or geography. Local SHAP and LIME explain one applicant, which supplies the three adverse reasons in a notice. When the two local methods disagree the app says so.

Honest limits

The target is claim occurrence, not underwriting approval, severity or fraud. Reference policies are rebuilt from the fitted preprocessor and group sizes, because the dataset is not bundled. Exposure dominates the model, which suggests it should be an actuarial offset. This is an educational prototype, not a certified pricing system.
